## Decide what it should say before designing

The title should name a concrete idea. “Important update” does not explain what changed; “My app now exports your expenses” does. This is a writing example: use a promise your project actually fulfills.

Secondary text can explain who it helps or what readers will find when opening the post. Remove it if it merely repeats the title. A signature identifies the brand without needing to compete with the main message.

| Element | Its job | What to avoid |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Title | Identify the topic | Vague or excessively long wording |
| Secondary text | Add necessary context | Repeating the entire title |
| Photo | Show the subject or support the idea | A background that prevents reading |
| Logo or signature | Identify the author or brand | Becoming the largest element without a reason |

## Create a cover with Antrapost

1. Choose square, portrait or story format.
2. Write a short title and inspect the preview.
3. Add secondary text only when it contributes something.
4. Position the text at the top, middle or bottom.
5. Use a background color or select a photo from your device.
6. Adjust framing and darkening when using a photo.
7. Add an optional logo or signature and download the cover as PNG.

The editor’s formats are 1080 × 1080, 1080 × 1350 and 1080 × 1920. Then check how previews are cropped on your destination platform. A complete design may need a different composition for another aspect ratio.

## Contrast: inspect the area behind the text

Photographs contain bright and dark areas. White text on a white shirt can disappear even when the rest of the photograph is dark.

The photo-darkening control helps separate the message from the background. Use enough to make the title readable without turning the image into a dark blur. You can also change the crop, move the text or choose a photograph with less detail behind the title.

For solid backgrounds, the editor warns about low text contrast. That assistance does not replace inspecting the image at a small size: test the cover at phone size before calling it finished.

## An example for announcing a demo

For a portfolio application, choose a recognizable action in the screenshot: recording an expense, filtering results or viewing a summary. Avoid placing the whole interface behind a long title.

You can separate the cover from the technical detail. The first image introduces the problem, and another slide explains your solution. The [carousel guide](/en/create-instagram-carousel-from-text-for-free) helps organize that explanation without overloading the cover.

Do not add performance, user or savings figures you have not measured. A specific, demonstrable title is more useful than a huge claim without evidence.

## Photos and logos are optional

You can work with color and text alone. If adding a logo, the editor accepts JPG, PNG or WebP. Existing transparency usually helps it blend with the background, but the tool does not remove backgrounds.

If you need another logo format, see [what happens when converting PNG, JPG and WebP](/en/convert-png-jpg-webp-preserve-transparency). Changing an extension does not make an opaque image transparent.

## Frequently asked questions

### Can I create a cover without paying or using a photo?

Yes. Use a background color, add your text and download the PNG. A photograph and logo are optional.

### Why does my title not fit?

Space is limited. Shorten the title or secondary text rather than putting an entire explanation onto one cover.

### Does the same image work on every network?

Do not assume so. Check the destination’s format, framing and previews, especially when moving between square, portrait and story formats.
