"I want to be a programmer, but I don't know where to start." The usual consequence is to go around for months without really advancing.

This article is that map. A realistic roadmap to move from zero to your first job as a developer in 2026: what to learn, in what order, how much time to devote to it and, above all, how to know you are ready to apply. It does not promise "job in 3 months" (that is rarely honest), but it does promise a clear path by which you will know exactly where you are.

## Before starting: the right mentality

The technical roadmap matters, but what separates those who come from those who leave is mentality. Two ideas to record you:

*   It is a marathon, not a sprint. Daily consistency beats weekend marathons. Half an hour a day, every day, exceeds ten hours on a loose Sunday.
*   Discomfort is the signal. When something costs, you are learning. If everything is easy for you, you are not growing. I develop it in [The importance of the growth mindset](/en/growth-mindset-for-personal-and-professional-development).

## Phase 1: Foundations (weeks 1–6)

Don't start with a framework. It begins with what everything else holds.

1.  A basic language. Python or JavaScript. Learn variables, conditionals, loops, functions and basic data structures.
2.  Programming logic. Solving small problems is more important than syntax. Reinforce it with the [complete guide to logic and data structures](/en/programming-logic-and-data-structures-guide).
3.  Terminal and Git from day one. Don't leave it for later: the sooner you integrate it, the better.

If you doubt which stack to choose, I simplify it in [The best technologies to start programming in 2026](/en/best-technologies-to-start-programming).

## Phase 2: Build for the web (weeks 7 to 14)

Now that you think logically, it is time to build things that others can see and use.

1.  HTML and CSS. Structure and style. Practice replicating real designs.
2.  JavaScript in the browser. Manipulate the DOM, events, consume an API.
3.  Your first real project. Small, but finished and deployed online. This is where knowledge solidifies.

## Phase 3: Specialization and a framework (weeks 15 to 26)

With a solid base, it chooses a path and deepens. Not before.

*   Frontend: a framework like React (and then Next.js).
*   Backend: Node, Django or FastAPI, more databases (SQL).
*   Fullstack: a little of both, without obsessing about mastering everything.

Don't know which one to choose? I compare it thoroughly in [frontend, backend or fullstack: which to choose in 2026](/en/frontend-backend-or-full-stack-which-to-choose), and you have specific routes per tool in [The most used frameworks in 2026 and their learning path](/en/popular-frameworks-and-learning-paths).

## Phase 4: Portfolio and preparation (weeks 27 to 36)

Knowing how to program is not enough: it must be demonstrated. This phase is the one that most people skip and the one that gets the most interviews.

1.  2 or 3 solid projects, completed, deployed and that you can explain. Choose well: I help you in [projects that really work for your CV](/en/portfolio-projects-that-strengthen-your-developer-resume).
2.  A portfolio and a care GitHub. Repos with good README, clean code and projects that you know how to explain: it is your cover letter to any recruiter.
3.  Preparation of interviews. Review fundamentals and practice explaining your reasoning aloud, something key in the [technical assessments](/en/how-to-prepare-for-coding-interviews).

## How do I know I am ready to apply?

Don't wait to feel "ready", that feeling never comes. You are ready when:

*   You can build a small project from scratch without following a tutorial.
*   You know how to find and solve mistakes on your own.
*   You have 2 or 3 projects that you can explain with certainty.
*   You understand what you write, even if you use help to write it.

If you check those boxes, you postulate now. You will learn a lot in the interviews themselves.

## Frequently asked questions

How long does it take to get the first job?
With constant dedication, between 6 and 12 months is a realistic range for many people, although it varies greatly according to your daily time and your context. Do not trust who promises you a guaranteed job in 3 months.

Do I need a university degree?
It is not mandatory. The market values what you know how to demonstrate: projects, portfolio and how you solve problems. A title helps some companies, but many developers come in being self-taught with a good portfolio.

Frontend, backend or fullstack to start?
For the first job, choosing a path and doing it right is usually better than staying halfway through everything. Frontend has a visual and motivating input; backend is highly demanded. I compare it in [frontend, backend or fullstack](/en/frontend-backend-or-full-stack-which-to-choose).

Should I learn AI in this roadmap?
Use it as support to learn faster, but do not make it the goal until you have a basis. First fundamentals; then, if you are interested, specialize. How to use it without becoming dependent is in [How to truly learn in AI era](/en/learn-programming-with-ai-without-becoming-dependent).

## Conclusion: the map is simple, the road is yours

The roadmap from zero to contracted is not a secret: fundamentals, build, specialize and demonstrate it. The difficult thing is not to understand the map, it is to walk it without abandoning it when frustration arrives (and will arrive).

You do not need the perfect path, you need to start yours and be constant. Take the first step today, elusive [The most common mistakes when learning to program](/en/common-mistakes-when-learning-to-code) and, in a few months, you will look back surprised at how far you got.
