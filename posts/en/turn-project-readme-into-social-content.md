You finish a project, write its README and then struggle to present it outside GitHub. Posting “I built an app with React” identifies your tool but does not explain why someone should try it. **Your README can become a source of useful social content**, without turning into copied installation instructions.

This guide extracts a demonstrable story and prepares a carousel, cover and short caption. We will use [Antrapost’s free tools](https://www.antrapost.com/en/tools?utm_source=ulises_io&utm_medium=referral&utm_campaign=antrapost_guides&utm_content=en_convertir-readme-proyecto-contenido-redes), one of my projects, which require no registration for this work.

## Extract four pieces from the README

Before designing, identify the problem, a visible action, a technical decision and a link for trying the project. If one is missing, your next step may be better documentation rather than an announcement.

| README section | Question it answers | Content you can create |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Goal | Who is this project for? | Announcement opening |
| Demo and screenshots | What can someone do? | Cover or demonstration image |
| Decisions | What did you choose and why? | A carousel explanation |
| How to try it | What is the next step? | Link and final action |

You do not need to describe every dependency. Someone trying an application has different questions from someone cloning its repository.

## Step 1: choose an action you can demonstrate

For an expense app, “manage information” is too broad. “Record an expense and see the monthly total” offers something observable.

Open the demo and perform the action. Use example data and avoid showing credentials, personal information or private dashboards. If the project is not deployed, you can show a local screenshot without presenting it as an available public demo.

The [resume project guide](/en/portfolio-projects-that-strengthen-your-developer-resume) helps prepare a demo and README you can defend during an interview.

## Step 2: prepare a readable screenshot

A screenshot of your entire desktop often contains more noise than information. Keep the area explaining the action and check that important text is readable on a phone.

[Antrapost’s resizer](https://www.antrapost.com/en/tools/image-resizer?utm_source=ulises_io&utm_medium=referral&utm_campaign=antrapost_guides&utm_content=en_convertir-readme-proyecto-contenido-redes) can crop or retain the full image with a background. If the file is too large, use the compressor after deciding on framing.

Do not alter a screenshot to show a nonexistent feature. A composition can present material better without inventing the result.

## Step 3: explain one decision in a few slides

One possible sequence is problem, chosen approach, difficulty, verification and next step. Filling every slide is unnecessary; each should contribute an idea.

With the [carousel maker](https://www.antrapost.com/en/tools/carousel-maker?utm_source=ulises_io&utm_medium=referral&utm_campaign=antrapost_guides&utm_content=en_convertir-readme-proyecto-contenido-redes), write one block per slide and separate them with `---`. The editor supports up to ten and downloads individual PNGs or a ZIP.

For example, explain why you also validate data on the server. Show the error you wanted to prevent and how you checked the behavior. “I followed best practices” does not identify your decision; a concrete case makes it assessable.

## Step 4: write an announcement without duplicating everything

The caption need not transcribe the carousel. Introduce the problem, preview the explanation’s value and offer a concrete action: try the demo, read the README or discuss a decision.

The [caption counter](https://www.antrapost.com/en/tools/caption-character-counter?utm_source=ulises_io&utm_medium=referral&utm_campaign=antrapost_guides&utm_content=en_convertir-readme-proyecto-contenido-redes) helps compare drafts. Its length target is personal, not an official network limit or a quality assessment.

Choose one primary action. Three links and five invitations can make the reader’s next step less clear.

## Step 5: retain evidence and check the destination

Save the original text, exported images and the link to the version you showed. Free tools process content in the browser and do not retain drafts after you leave.

Open the link in a private window and inspect what someone without your session will see. Then use the [publishing checklist](https://www.antrapost.com/en/tools/post-checklist?utm_source=ulises_io&utm_medium=referral&utm_campaign=antrapost_guides&utm_content=en_convertir-readme-proyecto-contenido-redes) to inspect files, accounts and timing.

## What not to turn into a promise

Do not present a copied tutorial as an original architecture or a small demo as a system tested at scale. Explain what you learned, acknowledge sources and describe the work you actually did.

If you used AI, explain what you verified and which decisions you can discuss. Sharing a project should help you talk about it rather than create a story you cannot support later.

## Frequently asked questions

### Do I need a huge README?

No. You need enough information to understand, run and evaluate the project. Its length depends on what it needs to explain, not a fixed word count.

### Can I prepare everything without an Antrapost account?

You can use the free tools to create and download materials without registration. Publishing or scheduling inside the product is a separate workflow with its own terms.

### What if my project is still incomplete?

Share a concrete milestone and its limitations. Explain what works and what remains rather than announcing a finished solution.
