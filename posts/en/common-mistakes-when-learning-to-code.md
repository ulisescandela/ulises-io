Learning to program can become frustrating when the same avoidable mistakes keep you from making progress. Before deciding that programming is not for you, look at how you practice. These patterns are common, and you can change them.

In this article I review the most common mistakes when learning to program, those that I myself committed and those that I see repeated throughout the world. They are not problems of intelligence or talent: they are problems of method. Change the method and change the result. Let's go for them.

## 1. Collecting courses instead of finishing one

Error number one. You start a course, in the third class you see another that seems better, you jump, and thus accumulate ten courses at 20%. You feel that "you are learning" because you are busy, but you do not consolidate anything.

How to avoid it: choose one resource and finish it before touching another. Consistency in a single source is worth more than variety. Less open eyelashes, more real progress.

## 2. Watching tutorials without writing code

Watching someone program on YouTube is comfortable and addictive, and gives you a false feeling that you understand it. But looking is not doing. Learning happens when your fingers write the code and you face the error.

How to avoid it: for every minute of tutorial, you dedicate several to write code yourself. It is the "tutorial hell" trap, and it has a solution: the entire development in [How to get out of the tutorial hell](/en/how-to-escape-tutorial-hell).

## 3. Trying to learn everything at once

HTML, CSS, JavaScript, React, Node, databases, Docker... all the first month. The result is abrupt and zero depth. The brain does not fix so many new things at once.

How to avoid it: one thing at a time, and in order. If you are just starting, follow a clear path like the one of [The best technologies to start programming in 2026](/en/best-technologies-to-start-programming) instead of jumping to the most advanced.

## 4. Skipping the fundamentals to reach the “fun” parts

Many run to build a beautiful website without understanding variables, loops or how the data is structured. It works for a while, until the first moderately complex problem leaves them blocked without knowing why.

How to avoid it: dedicate real time to the base. It is the least attractive and the most profitable. Reinforce it with the [complete guide to logic and data structures](/en/programming-logic-and-data-structures-guide): that investment holds everything else.

## 5. Giving up at the first error

The beginner sees a message of error and feels that he did something wrong, that "it does not work for this". The experienced programmer sees the same error and knows that it is a normal part of the job: half of programming is wrong and correct.

How to avoid it: change your relationship with mistakes. Read them calmly (say more than you think), look for them, and celebrate when you solve them. Learning to debug is a skill in itself, and one of the most valuable.

## 6. Comparing yourself with developers who have years of experience

You open X or LinkedIn, you see someone building something impressive and you feel like you are late. But you are comparing your 30th day with someone else's year 5. This comparison only serves to discourage you.

How to avoid it: compare yourself with your "self" of a month ago. Progress is personal. All those you admire today were clumsy beginners at some point.

## 7. Do not build anything of your own

You finish courses and exercises, but you never build something of your own from start to finish. And it is right there, in the project itself, where all the pieces come together and knowledge becomes solid.

How to avoid it: as soon as possible, build a small project of truth, even if it is imperfect. If you lack ideas, look at [projects that really work for your CV](/en/portfolio-projects-that-strengthen-your-developer-resume).

## Frequently asked questions

Is it normal to feel lost when you start programming?
Totally. The feeling of being overwhelmed and "not understanding anything" is universal in the first weeks. It is not a sign that you do not serve: it is a sign that you are learning something genuinely new. It persists and passes.

How long does it take to stop making these mistakes?
Method errors disappear as soon as you know them and apply the antidote, almost immediately. What takes time is the technical ease, but avoiding these failures you advance much faster than most.

Is it wrong to use AI when I start?
No, if you use it to understand and not to solve for you. The serious mistake is to rely on it to skip the effort to think. How to use it well I explain it in [How to truly learn in AI era](/en/learn-programming-with-ai-without-becoming-dependent).

What if I have already been months and I feel that I am not moving forward?
It is usually one of these mistakes disguised, usually not building anything of its own or jumping between resources. It reviews the list with honesty and applies the corresponding antidote.

## Conclusion: it is not talent, it is method

If you recognized yourself in several of these mistakes, good luck: you just found out why you were not moving forward, and everyone has a solution. Learning to program is not a matter of a magical talent that some have and others do not. It is a matter of method, constancy and not surrendering to the normal frustration of the principle.
