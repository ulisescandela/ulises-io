In the technology industry, especially in high profiles such as senior developers, tech leads, managers or directors, technical skills are only a part of success. Soft skills (those interpersonal and emotional skills) are crucial to lead teams, manage complex projects and build a sustainable career. In this article, I will share practical tips to increase your soft skills, focusing on how to apply them in high-level IT environments.

## 1. Develop effective communication

Communication is the basis of any leadership role. In IT, this means not only explaining technical concepts to non-technical ones, but also articulating strategic visions and constructive feedback.

- Practice active listening: In meetings, focus on understanding before responding. Use techniques such as paraphrasing what you hear to confirm understanding.
- Adapt your language: speak in accessible terms. For example, instead of "implementing a machine learning algorithm", say "using AI to predict patterns in data".
- Exercise: Record your presentations and analyze your clarity. Join Toastmasters groups or practice with colleagues.

## 2. Cultivating Leadership and Mentoring

As a senior professional, leading means inspiring and guiding, not just directing. IT leadership involves fostering innovation and team growth.

- Be a mentor: Share knowledge without expecting reciprocity. It organizes pair programming sessions or constructive code reviews.
- Inspires trust: Leads for example, not by authority. It shows vulnerability admitting errors to build resilient teams.
- Exercise: Identify a junior in your team and dedicate weekly time to their development. It measures the impact on their progress.

## 3. Improve Teamwork and Collaboration

IT projects are rarely individual. In high roles, coordinating multifunctional teams is key.

- It encourages diversity of opinions: In brainstorming, it encourages everyone to contribute, especially introverts.
- Solve conflicts: Use techniques such as "I feel, I need" to address disagreements without scaling.
- Exercise: In your next project, rotate roles (technical leader, facilitator, etc.) so that everyone experiences different perspectives.

## 4. Manage Time and Productivity

Time management becomes critical in high profiles, where you balance multiple priorities.

- Prioritize with frameworks: Use Eisenhower Matrix or OKRs to distinguish the urgent from the important.
- Avoid multitasking: Focus on one task at a time; multitasking reduces IT quality.
- Exercise: Review your week: How much time do you spend on meetings vs. deep work? It adjusts to dedicate 2-3 hour blocks to complex tasks.

## 5. Develop emotional intelligence (EQ)

EQ is essential to manage the stress of tight deadlines and constant technological changes.

- Self-knowledge: Reflect on your emotional triggers. How do you react under pressure?
- Empathy: Put yourself in the shoes of colleagues or stakeholders. In IT, this means understanding technical frustrations or commercial pressures.
- Exercise: Keep a diary of emotions for a week. Identify patterns and strategies to regulate them.

## 6. Strengthen your network and visibility

In high profiles, networking is not optional; it is strategic.

- Build genuine relationships: Attend conferences, meetups or webinars. Not only to talk, but to listen and learn.
- Share knowledge: Write articles, give talks or contribute to open source. This increases your visibility and credibility.
- Exercise: Identify 3 key contacts in your network and schedule a monthly call to discuss trends in IT.

## 7. Embracing Continuous Learning and Adaptability

Technology evolves rapidly; adaptability is a premium soft skill.

- Maintain a growth mindset: See failures as opportunities. In IT, this means experimenting with new technologies without fear.
- Learn intentionally: Take time to read about leadership, psychology or adjacent industries.
- Exercise: Select a new tool or methodology (e.g. Advanced agile) and apply it in a small project.

## Frequently asked questions

Do soft skills matter more than technical skills?
It is not a competition: they complement each other. Techniques open the door for you; soft skills make you ascend. In fact, [Being a good programmer does not earn you more](/en/why-good-programmers-do-not-always-earn-more) alone, communication and visibility weigh heavily.

How do I develop soft skills if I am introverted?
Start with the asynchronous: write clear documentation, comment on PRs with empathy, share written learning. Communication is not just public speaking.

What soft skill do I work first?
Communication. It is the basis of all the others and the one that is most noticeable in interviews and on a day-to-day basis. If you are looking for a broader vision of how to stand out, I leave you my [guide to stand out in tech in 2026](/en/how-to-stand-out-in-tech).

## Conclusion

Increasing soft skills is not a linear process, but it is essential to advance in the IT career. It begins with self-knowledge and consistent practice. Remember, the best investments are in yourself. If you apply these tips, you will not only improve professionally, but you will also contribute to more effective teams and more innovative organizations. Which soft skill will you work first?
