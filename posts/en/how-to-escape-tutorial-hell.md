You know the feeling: you have been watching tutorials for months, you have completed entire courses, you understand everything while the instructor explains it... but when you open the editor with a blank screen, you do not know where to start. That is the "tutorial hell", and it is one of the most frustrating places in which anyone who learns to program can get trapped.

The trap is cruel because it feels like progress. You are busy, you learn concepts, you mark lessons as completed. But the ability to build on your own does not come. In this article I explain why it happens and, above all, a concrete method to get out of it and start programming for real.

## Why tutorials can keep you stuck

The tutorial hell is not your fault: it is designed to hook you up. Understanding why it happens is the first step to escape.

*   You confuse recognizing with remembering. Following someone who has already made all the decisions is easy. Making those decisions from scratch is hard, and it is what a tutorial never forces you to do.
*   The comfort zone is addictive. A tutorial is comfortable and predictable: it never fails completely. The blank screen is terrifying precisely because there you can be wrong.
*   False productivity. Completing lessons releases a small reward. You feel that you advance although your real ability to build does not move.

It is one of the [Most common mistakes when learning to program](/en/common-mistakes-when-learning-to-code), and also the most sticky.

## The method to get out: building with discomfort

The only way out of the hell tutorial is to program without anyone taking you by the hand. But doing it suddenly overwhelms. This is the gradual way that works.

### Step 1: Finish the tutorial, but break it

The next time you follow a tutorial, do not limit yourself to copying. Change it: add functionality that is not in the video, modify the design, change the data. The moment you step out of the script, your brain begins to really learn.

### Step 2: Rebuild without looking

When you finish a guided project, close it and build it again from scratch without seeing the tutorial. You are going to get stuck, and that is exactly the point: every jam you solve is a real learning leap that no video gives you.

### Step 3: Build something that does not have a tutorial

The definitive step. Choose a small project that interests you and for which there is no step-by-step guide. A tool you need, something personal. It will force you to put together pieces, search the documentation and make decisions yourself. There you stop being a spectator. If you lack ideas, look at [projects that really work for your CV](/en/portfolio-projects-that-strengthen-your-developer-resume).

### Step 4: Use documentation, not another tutorial

When you get stuck, resist the temptation to look for "another tutorial of the same". Instead, go to the official documentation or look for the specific error. Learning to find answers on your own is, perhaps, the most important skill of all.

## Table: inside the tutorial hell vs. outside it

|Inside the tutorial hell|Out of the tutorial hell|
|---|---|
|Copies line by line|You make your own decisions|
|Non-stop courses chains|You build your own projects|
|Looking for "another tutorial" to jam|Search in the documentation|
|You understand seeing, not doing|You know how to build from scratch|
|Sensation of progress without real progress|Slow but solid progress|

## The role of AI: use it carefully

In 2026 there is a new and more dangerous version of the hell tutorial: asking AI to write the code for you. It feels even more productive, and further stunts. Copying from an AI without understanding is a tutorial hell with steroids.

Use AI to explain why something fails or to ask for clues, never to solve the whole problem. The difference between learning and stagnating the detail in [How to truly learn in AI era](/en/learn-programming-with-ai-without-becoming-dependent).

## Frequently asked questions

Are tutorials bad then?
No, they are excellent for a first contact. The problem is not tutorials, it is to stay alone in them. Use them as a starting point and leave them as soon as you understand the basics, building on your own.

How do I know if I am in the tutorial hell?
Simple test: close everything and try to build something small from scratch. If you can't start without a guide, you are inside. Nothing happens: now you know how to get out.

How long will it take me to leave?
As soon as you build your first project from start to finish, you are already out. It can be a matter of weeks if you apply the method. The important thing is to take the leap to the blank screen, even if it is afraid.

What if I get stuck all the time when building alone?
It is normal and it is a good sign: it means that you are in the area where you learn. Getting stuck and solving it is the real work of programming. Reduce the size of the project if necessary, but do not return to the comfort of the tutorial.

## Conclusion: the blank screen is the goal, not the enemy

Quitting the hell tutorial comes down to an uncomfortable but liberating idea: you have to build without anyone telling you how. It is afraid, you are going to get stuck, and precisely that is why it works. Each problem you solve only brings you closer to being a real programmer.

Stop collecting tutorials and start building today, even if it is something tiny and imperfect. That blank screen that intimidates you today is actually where your autonomy begins. And from there, the entire [roadmap from zero to contracted](/en/developer-roadmap-from-beginner-to-hired) becomes much easier to travel.
