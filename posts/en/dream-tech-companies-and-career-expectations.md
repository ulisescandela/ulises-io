We all have these companies in mind. Google. Meta. Apple. Netflix. Amazon. Microsoft.

They are the dreams of most developers. The ones you see on LinkedIn with millions of followers, the ones everyone wants to have in the CV, the ones that promise six-figure salaries and movie benefits.

But here comes brutal realism: few people really arrive.

## The elephant in the room: the hiring process

Look at the true figures:

- Google receives more than 5 million requests per year and hires less than 1%
- Meta has 1.51.5 million applicants per year, accepts similar percentages
- Apple, Netflix, and the others: exactly the same

Do you know what that means in real numbers? If 5 million people apply and they hire, let's say, 20,000 people a year... we speak of 0.4% acceptance.

To compare: Harvard has ▁aceptación3.2% acceptance.

I'm not telling you not to try. I tell you to understand the game you are playing.

## Why so few applicants get in

It is not only that they are selective. They are looking for a very specific thing:

1. Demonstrable track record: open source repositories, projects in GitHub with thousands of stars, or experience already in top startups
2. Network: meeting people inside (or having gone to elite universities)
3. Timing: being available when they open position in your city/remote
4. Pure luck: because with 5 million candidates, many people are just as capable as you are left out for timing

And the uncomfortable part: many people are simply not going to arrive, no matter how hard they try. It is not fair, but it is true.

## Other career paths worth considering

And when I say "almost so good", I mean money. In prestige perhaps not, but in money...

### Companies that pay as FAANG:

- Stripe: competitive salaries, extraordinary environment, remote-friendly
- Notion: scale-up that pays just like the big ones
- Figma: $ 200k-$ 400k+ for Senior Engineers, without jokes
- Airbnb: it is still accessible and salaries are public (Levels.fyi)
- Datadog: infrastructure company, aggressive salaries
- Canva: exponential growth, FAANG-level salaries
- Mistral AI / AI startups: if you want adventure, these places pay base salaries $ 150k-$200k + juicy equity

### The secret: well-funded startups

This is where the REAL opportunity is. Series B/C startups:

- Fresh money from top tier investors
- Competitive wages (base $130k-$180k + equity)
- Equity that could be worth 10x in 5-7 years
- Freedom to work on interesting problems
- Less filters than FAANG (but still rigorous)

Catch? More risk. The startup could fail. But if you are right... that equity changes your life.

## Let's talk about real money

Because we are going to be honest: many of us care about money.

A Senior Engineer in FAANG today:

- Base: 200k-250k
- Bonus: $ 50k-$ 100k
- Stock (annual): $ 200k-$ 500k (distributed in 4 years)
- Annual total: $450k-$850k

Sounds incredible. And it is. But:

1. The stock is distributed, you do not receive everything anymore.
2. They pay brutal federal taxes in the USA (440%)
3. Many of these positions require being in the Bay Area (expensive as bullshit)

A Senior In Startup Series B:

- Base: $150k-$180k
- Bonus: $20k-$40k
- Equity: 0.1%-0.5% (depending on the role)
- If the startup succeeds: that equity is worth $ 500k-$5M in 5 years

The point? You don't need FAANG to win six figures. But you need to be willing to take a risk.

## Realism in perspective

Here is my take, clean and without sugar:

1. If your goal is to work on Google/Goal: go ahead, try. But have a solid Plan B. Competition is unhealthy.

2. If your goal is to win well: there are 100 paths. FAANG is one, but there are startups, European companies with visa sponsorship, remote work global, premium agencies...

3. If your goal is job satisfaction + money: look at startups in emerging markets or Series A/B. Many times you have more impact and better balance.

4. If your goal is technical growth: yes, it could depend on where you are. But there are startups where you learn more than in FAANG (because you have to do everything).

## The uncomfortable truth

Working in a FAANG is not going to make you happier if you are not interested in the job.

Working in startup is not going to break your bank if you negotiate equity well.

Not working in FAANG is not going to limit you if you have fundamental good and solid portfolio.

The tech market is large. Very big.

## Conclusion: Aim high, but be smart

Applies to Google. It applies to Meta. Applies to Netflix. But it also applies to those startups on Levels.fyi that pay $ 200k base and have equity promising.

Don't lower standards, but don't confuse the dream with real opportunities.

Dreamed companies are for some. But being well, earning well, learning well, enjoying your work ... that is within the reach of many more people than you think.

You just need to be willing to look beyond the logo.
