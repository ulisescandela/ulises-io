I have seen technically skilled developers struggle to progress while others build strong careers with more modest technical ability. Skill alone does not explain every outcome. The way you approach learning, feedback and setbacks also matters.

You can master languages and frameworks, but if your mentality is wrong, you are going to crash into an invisible ceiling. This article is about how the growth mindset helps you constantly improve programming, overcome mistakes and grow professionally.


## Mindset is the operating system of your life

Think about it like this: your mentality is like the operating system of a computer. You can have the best hardware in the world, but if the OS is corrupt or outdated, nothing will work well.

Your technical skills are hardware. Your mindset is the software that decides how you use that hardware.

### Two people, same circumstances, opposite results

Imagine two junior developers entering the same company on the same day:

Developer A: Receives negative feedback in its first code review. Think: "I am a fraud, I don't serve for this, everyone is better than me". He gets defensive, avoids asking for help so as not to appear incompetent, and stagnates repeating the same mistakes.

Developer B: Receives the same negative feedback. Think, "OK, there are things I don't know. What can I learn from this?". Ask, investigate, implement the suggestions. In three months, their code reviews are exemplary.

Same situation. The same company. Same feedback. Completely different results.

The only variable that changed was mentality.

## Fixed vs. growth mindset

Carol Dweck, a psychologist at Stanford, identified two fundamental types of mindset that determine how we face challenges:

### Fixed mentality

People with fixed mindsets believe that their basic qualities (intelligence, talent, skills) are fixed traits that cannot change significantly.

Signs of fixed mentality:

- "I'm not good for X" (and point, without intention to change)
- You avoid challenges because failure would confirm that "you are not enough"
- You get frustrated when something doesn't go well at first
- You see effort as a sign that you have no natural talent
- You feel threatened by the success of others
- Ignoring critical feedback or putting yourself on the defensive
- You give up easily when things get hard

Typical Phrases:
- "I don't work for the frontend"
- "Mathematics is not mine"
- "You understand it or you do not understand it"
- "He has natural talent, I don't"

### Growth mindset

People with a growth mindset believe that their skills can be developed through effort, good strategies and feedback from others.

Signs of growth mindset:

- "I'm still not good for X" (the "still" is key)
- You look for challenges because they are opportunities to learn
- You see errors as information, not as sentences
- Effort is the path to mastery
- You are inspired by the success of others
- You seek and appreciate critical feedback
- Persistence in the face of obstacles

Typical Phrases:
- "How can I improve in this?"
- "Interesting, what can I learn from this mistake?"
- "If he could, I can learn too"
- " Give me honest feedback, I want to improve"

## Why mentality matters more in tech

The technology industry has characteristics that make the mentality even more critical:

### 1. Constant change

What you learn today can be obsolete in 3 years. React did not exist 12 years ago. TypeScript was optional 5. LLMs changed everything in 2 years.

With a fixed mentality, every technological change is a threat to your "expert" identity. With a growth mindset, each change is an opportunity to reinvent yourself.

### 2. Amplified imposter syndrome

In tech, there is always someone who knows more. There is always a new framework that you do not know. There is always a problem that you cannot solve.

The fixed mentality turns this into paralysis: "I don't belong here". The growth mindset normalizes it: "There will always be more to learn, and that's fine".

### 3. Public and constant feedback

Code reviews, pull requests, technical discussions... Your work is constantly exposed to the scrutiny of others.

The fixed mentality makes each critic feel like a personal attack. The growth mindset processes it as data to improve.

### 4. The pressure of the "10x developer"

The industry has romanticized the idea of the genius programmer who solves everything alone. This creates unrealistic expectations.

The reality is that the best teams are not full of solitary geniuses, but of people who collaborate, learn together and continuously improve.

## How your mentality affects your career

Let's go to the concrete. Your mindset has a direct impact on every aspect of your professional development:

### In the interviews

Fixed mentality: "If I don't solve this problem, I will be left like an idiot. Better not say anything if I am not 100% sure."

Growth mindset: "I am going to think aloud, show my process. If I get stuck, I ask for clues. The important thing is to demonstrate how I approach problems."

The irony is that interviewers do not expect perfection. They want to see how you think, how you handle uncertainty, how you respond to clues. The growth mindset allows you to show exactly that.

### On a daily basis

Fixed mentality: Avoid tasks outside your comfort zone. You specialize so much that you become irreplaceable... but also immobile.

Growth mindset: You raise your hand for challenging projects. Requests to rotate between teams. You learn adjacent technologies. You become versatile.

### In the errors

Fixed mentality: A bug in production is a personal catastrophe. You look for excuses, minimize, or spiral guilt.

Growth mindset: A bug is a symptom. What failed in the process? What test was missing? What can I do so that it does not happen again?

### In promotions

Fixed mentality: "If I deserve a promotion, my boss should realize it alone. Asking her is arrogant."

Growth mindset: "I'm going to have a clear conversation about my growth. What do I need to demonstrate? What skills to develop? Give me a roadmap."

### In learning

Fixed mentality: "I am already senior, I already know what I need to know. That of the courses is for juniors."

Growth mindset: "I have been in this for 15 years and every day I learn something new. The day I stop learning, I begin to become obsolete."

## Mindset in personal growth

This is not just about work. Your mentality affects every area of your life:

### Relationships

Fixed mentality: "That's me, take me or leave me. I am not going to change for anyone."

Growth mindset: "Relationships require work. How can I be a better partner, friend, colleague?"

### Health

Fixed mentality: "I am not a person of exercise. My metabolism is slow. It is genetic."

Growth mindset: "My body responds to the stimulus I give it. I can improve my health with consistent habits."

### Finance

Fixed mentality: "I am not good with money. I will never understand investments."

Growth mindset: "Personal finance is a skill like any other. I can learn the basics."

### Creativity

Fixed mentality: "I am not creative. That is for artists."

Growth mindset: "Creativity is a muscle. It is exercised. I can develop it."

## Signs that your mentality is holding you back

Make an honest inventory. Do you recognize any of these patterns?

### 1. Feedback avoidance

If you are uncomfortable asking for opinions about your work, or become defensive when you receive them, your fixed mentality is operating.

### 2. Destructive comparison

If the success of others makes you feel worse about yourself instead of inspiring you, there is work to be done.

### 3. Paralysis by analysis

If you spend more time planning than executing because "it has to come out perfect", your fear of failure is winning.

### 4. Blame external factors

If there is always an external reason why things do not work (the market, your boss, the company, the economy), you are avoiding looking in the mirror.

### 5. Permanent comfort zone

If you haven't done something that scares you a little professionally for more than a year, you are stagnant.

### 6. Absolutist language

Pay attention to your words. "Always", "never", "can't", "this is how I am" are signs of fixed mentality.

## How to develop a growth mindset

The good news: mentality is not fixed (intentional irony). You can train it. Here are concrete strategies:

### 1. Change your internal dialogue

When you hear yourself thinking in absolute terms, add "still":

- "I do not understand Kubernetes" → "I still do not understand Kubernetes"
- "I'm not good at leading" → "I'm still not good at leading"
- "I don't know how to speak in public"  " "I still don't know how to speak in public"

This "still" opens the door to possibility.

### 2. Reframe failures

Whenever something goes wrong, ask yourself:

- What did I learn?
- What would make it different?
- What new information do I have now?

Failure is feedback, not verdict.

### 3. celebrates the process, not just the results

Instead of just celebrating when you achieve something, celebrate when:

- You tried something difficult (regardless of the result)
- Request feedback
- You persisted when you wanted to surrender
- Learn something new

### 4. Seeking challenges deliberately

Once a month, do something that makes you uncomfortable professionally:

- Give a talk
- Lead a meeting
- Contributes to an open source project
- Learn a technology outside your stack
- Write a technical article

Growth lives outside the comfort zone.

### 5. Surround yourself with a growth mindset

Mentalities are contagious. Search for people who:

- They talk about what they are learning
- They openly share their failures
- They genuinely celebrate the successes of others
- Give and receive feedback with maturity

Stay away from those who only complain and never act.

### 6. Study those you admire

Investigate the history of successful people in your field. You will discover that:

- All failed multiple times
- None was born knowing
- All invested years of deliberate practice
- Most attribute their success to effort, not talent

"Natural talent" is almost always invisible work.

### 7. Practice gratitude for challenges

This sounds cheesy, but it works. When you face a difficult problem, practice thinking: "How good I have the opportunity to solve this. It will make me better."

Reframing challenges as opportunities literally changes your physiological response to stress.

## Mindset in the context of business

Let's talk specifically about how this applies to work in a company:

### To grow in your current role

What those who stagnate think: "I do my job well. That should be enough."

What do those who grow up think: "How can I do my job in a way that creates more value? What problems can I solve that no one has asked me to solve?"

Those who grow do not wait for instructions. They identify opportunities and act.

### To get promotions

Promotions are not given for seniority. They occur when you show that you are already operating at the next level.

Mentality that stagnates: "When I am promoted, I will start doing things of that level."

Mentality that grows: "I'm going to start doing things of that level. The promotion will be formal recognition."

### To manage office policy

Yes, it exists. No, you cannot ignore it.

Naive mentality: "I just want to program. Politics does not interest me."

Realist mentality: "Organizations are social systems. Understanding how they work is part of being effective."

This does not mean being manipulative. It means understanding who makes decisions, what incentives they have, and how to communicate your value in a way that resonates.

### To give and receive feedback

As a recipient:
- Listening without interrupting
- Assumes good intention
- Ask for specific examples
- Thanks (although it hurts)
- Act on what is valid

As a donor:
- Be specific and timely
- Focus on behaviors, not personality
- It offers suggestions, not just criticisms
- Recognise the genuine positive

### To manage conflicts

Conflicts are inevitable. Your mentality determines whether you turn them into growth or resentment.

Defensive mentality: "I am right and the other is wrong."

Growth mindset: "There is a reason why the other thinks differently. What information does it have that I do not have? Where could I be wrong?"

## The cost of fixed mentality

Let's be clear about what is at stake:

### Professional cost

- Opportunities that you do not take out of fear
- Promotions that do not arrive because you do not expose yourself
- Skills that you do not develop because "they are not your thing"
- Professional relationships that you do not build because you avoid vulnerability

### Personal cost

- Chronic stress for feeling that you are never enough
- Relationships damaged by inability to receive feedback
- Wasted potential for not trying
- Repentance for the "what would have happened if...?"

### Long-term cost

In 10 years, the difference between someone with a fixed mindset and someone with a growth mindset is abysmal. Not because one is more intelligent, but because one has been constantly growing while the other has been protecting itself.

## Frequently asked questions

What is the growth mindset?
It is the belief that your abilities are developed with effort and practice, rather than being fixed. It completely changes how you face challenges, mistakes and feedback.

How does mentality affect my tech career?
A lot. It often weighs more than pure talent: in fact, [Being a good programmer does not earn you more](/en/why-good-programmers-do-not-always-earn-more) if your mindset slows you down. Your willingness to learn and expose yourself defines your ceiling.

Can a fixed mentality be changed?
Yes. You were not born with it; you built it, and you can reconstruct it with deliberate practice. Pair it with specific skills for [stand out in the industry](/en/how-to-stand-out-in-tech).

## Conclusion

Your mentality is not something you were born with. It is something you have built throughout your life, influenced by your experiences, your environment and the stories you have told about yourself.

And just as you built it, you can rebuild it.

It is not easy. Changing thought patterns of years requires awareness, effort and patience. You're going to fall. You will have days where the fixed mentality wins. That is normal.

But every time you choose growth over protection, every time you reframe a failure as learning, every time you ask for feedback instead of avoiding it, you are training a new way of thinking.

And this new way of thinking is going to determine how far you get.

Not your talent. Not your intelligence. Not your circumstances.

Your mentality.

Choose well.
