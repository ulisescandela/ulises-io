A form with a name, an email address and a submit button can collect requests, but it does not necessarily let people book. If the client still needs to wait for a call to find out the time, coordination remains unfinished. **Designing a booking flow means making it explicit what is being confirmed.**

I will use [AntraPro](https://antrapro.app?utm_source=ulises_io&utm_medium=referral&utm_campaign=antrapro_guides&utm_content=en_disenar-pagina-reservas-negocio-servicios), one of my appointment software projects, as an example of an offering that brings together services, staff availability and online booking. The recommendations also apply if you are developing your own portfolio project.

## Start with a client decision

Before drawing screens, write down what someone needs to choose: service, duration, price where applicable, available professional, date and location. Do not hide an essential condition until after confirmation.

A name such as “premium service” may make sense to the business and tell a new visitor nothing. Describe what it includes. If an assessment is needed before quoting a price, call the flow “request an assessment” rather than promising a booking at a fixed price.

| Element | Client's question | Common mistake |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Service | What will I receive? | An ambiguous name |
| Duration | How much time should I set aside? | Showing only the start time |
| Professional | Who will serve me? | A selection without availability |
| Location | Where should I go? | A hidden address |
| Confirmation | Is my appointment registered? | A generic submitted message |

## Show times that make sense

Availability should reflect the selected service and a professional who can provide it. Displaying every gap before knowing the duration forces you to remove options later and makes the interface feel unreliable.

Consider the state with no available times too: explain whether the day is fully booked, the business is closed or another professional must be selected. An unexplained empty area does not help anyone choose. Let people change the date or service without unnecessarily erasing their personal information.

AntraPro's public offering lets clients choose a service, date and time through a booking page and receive an email confirmation. To evaluate any product, walk through that flow with your business's requirements; do not assume it works for you because a screenshot looks good.

## Design for someone on a phone

The page may open from Instagram or a message. Check that controls are easy to tap, the keyboard does not cover the main action and labels remain visible while typing. A placeholder disappears precisely when someone needs to remember which field they are filling in.

Associate labels with fields, identify errors alongside the affected value and do not communicate availability through color alone. If results change after a date selection, consider how that change is announced to someone using a screen reader.

Do not copy a complete desktop calendar onto a small screen. In a booking flow, selecting a day and time matters more than viewing every week of the month.

## Request information with a purpose

Collecting information “just in case” lengthens the form and increases what you must protect. Separate the information needed to provide the service from information you would only use for advertising. A booking should not silently become consent to marketing campaigns.

If your services involve sensitive information, check the applicable obligations before collecting it. A commercial appointment calendar does not, by itself, constitute an electronic medical record system.

## Make the confirmation useful afterward

The final message and email should let the client verify the service, date, time, professional and location, and explain how to contact the business if a change is needed. Avoid making people reconstruct those details from the previous form.

A confirmation sent when booking and a reminder sent before an appointment are different features. Do not advertise reminders, WhatsApp or online payments unless you have verified that the product offers them and under what conditions.

## How to evaluate the result

Test with someone unfamiliar with your business: ask them to book a specific service from their phone, then explain what they confirmed. Observe where they hesitate and what information they look for; do not tell them where to click. This reveals more than asking whether they liked the colors.
