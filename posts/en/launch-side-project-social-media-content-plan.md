Posting a side-project link and expecting everyone to understand its value leaves too much work to the reader. **A small launch needs to explain the problem, show how the project works and offer a clear way to try it.** It does not need to become a huge campaign.

This plan uses [Antrapost’s free tools](https://www.antrapost.com/en/tools?utm_source=ulises_io&utm_medium=referral&utm_campaign=antrapost_guides&utm_content=en_lanzar-side-project-redes-plan-publicaciones), one of my projects, to prepare materials. You can design and download without registration; using Antrapost to publish or schedule is a service separate from those tools.

## Start with a demo people can actually try

Before writing the announcement, open the link without your session and complete the main workflow. An attractive cover cannot fix a demo requiring your developer credentials or depending on a local server.

Decide what someone receives after clicking. Explain if they must create an account, pay or wait for access. If they will only see a repository, do not promise an application ready to use in a browser.

Check empty states and obvious errors too. You do not need to claim the product is perfect; you do need to distinguish available functionality from work still in progress.

## Prepare three pieces with different goals

| Piece | Question it answers | Primary action |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Introduction | Which problem does it solve, and for whom? | Open the demo or read the description |
| Explanation | How does an important part work? | Understand a decision or save the guide |
| Follow-up | What did you learn or change afterward? | Share a concrete observation |

You can distribute them throughout a week if that fits your audience. This is an organizational suggestion, not a universal frequency or schedule guaranteeing reach.

Avoid posting the same announcement three times with different covers. Each piece should teach readers something different.

## Piece 1: a cover and a concrete promise

The [cover maker](https://www.antrapost.com/en/tools/cover-maker?utm_source=ulises_io&utm_medium=referral&utm_campaign=antrapost_guides&utm_content=en_lanzar-side-project-redes-plan-publicaciones) combines a title, optional photo and your brand. Describe the benefit of a feature that already exists and inspect the image at a small size.

For an expense app, “See how much you spent this month” describes a use. “The best financial app” requires a comparison you probably have not made.

Add a caption explaining who might use it and what to do next. Check its length with the [caption counter](https://www.antrapost.com/en/tools/caption-character-counter?utm_source=ulises_io&utm_medium=referral&utm_campaign=antrapost_guides&utm_content=en_lanzar-side-project-redes-plan-publicaciones), without treating its editable target as an official network limit.

## Piece 2: an explanation demonstrating your work

Choose a project decision: validating data, organizing categories, handling an error or enabling export. Explain the problem, decision and how you verified the behavior.

With the [carousel maker](https://www.antrapost.com/en/tools/carousel-maker?utm_source=ulises_io&utm_medium=referral&utm_campaign=antrapost_guides&utm_content=en_lanzar-side-project-redes-plan-publicaciones), divide the explanation into blocks separated by `---`. Inspect every slide and download images before closing. You do not need all ten available slides.

If you are unsure what to extract from the project, the [README reuse guide](/en/turn-project-readme-into-social-content) helps identify demonstrable material.

## Piece 3: a follow-up without invented results

Explain what changed after launch or answer a question. If you received actual feedback, describe what you did with it without publishing personal information. If nobody has responded yet, discuss an improvement or problem you discovered yourself.

Do not turn clicks into paying customers or visits into satisfaction. Describe the measurement you actually have. Without configured analytics, you can inspect comments and test workflows without presenting nonexistent figures.

An honest update maintains the project’s story better than pretending there is enormous demand.

## Final review before publishing

Save text versions, files and the exact link. Check image order, crops and mobile readability. Then use [Antrapost’s checklist](https://www.antrapost.com/en/tools/post-checklist?utm_source=ulises_io&utm_medium=referral&utm_campaign=antrapost_guides&utm_content=en_lanzar-side-project-redes-plan-publicaciones) to review destinations, links and dates.

If managing multiple accounts, write down which belong to this launch. Distinguishing a personal account from a business account is a decision to verify, not half-remember after sending.

## Frequently asked questions

### Do I need to post every day for a launch?

There is no universal frequency. Prepare useful pieces you can sustain and choose a schedule appropriate for your project and audience.

### Can I announce a beta?

Yes, provided you explain it. State what works, what remains and how to report problems. A beta need not pretend to be a finished product.

### Do these tools write the launch for me?

No. The cover and carousel editors use your content. The checklist offers prompts to copy into a separate assistant for reviewing copy.
